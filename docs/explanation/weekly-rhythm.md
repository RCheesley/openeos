# How the pieces of the weekly rhythm connect

Empresadence is built around a rhythm, not a list of features. A team sets a direction, picks a few results for the quarter, and meets every week to keep them moving.

Your organisation may have renamed some words. This page uses the default names. See the [glossary](../reference/glossary.md).

## Three time spans

| Time span | Where it lives | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Years | Vision and Delivery Board | Where is the organisation going? |
| A quarter | Priorities and their Steps | What must be true in three months? |
| A week | Actions, Metrics Boards, Blockers and the Weekly Meeting | What needs doing now, and what is in the way? |

Each span feeds the next. The vision suggests the Priorities. The Priorities suggest this week's Actions.

## How the parts feed each other

```text
Vision and Delivery Board
        │  suggests
        ▼
   Priorities ──── broken into ───▶ Steps
        │
        │  reviewed every week in
        ▼
  Weekly Meeting ◀── numbers from ── Metrics Boards
        │                                  │
        │  works through                   │ a missed number can become
        ▼                                  ▼
     Blockers ◀──────────────────────── a Blocker
        │
        │  each one ends with
        ▼
     Actions ── reviewed at the next ──▶ Weekly Meeting
```

## The quarter: Priorities and Steps

A Priority is a result, not a task. It has one owner, a quarter and a due date. Its status is **On Track**, **Off Track**, **Complete** or **Dropped**.

Steps break a Priority into stages. Ticking them fills a progress bar, so the team can see how far along it is.

Each week the owner adds a check-in: on track or off track, with a short note. A Priority with no check-in for 7 days is marked **Check-in overdue**.

## The week: numbers, Actions and Blockers

A **Metrics Board** holds a handful of numbers with goals. Someone enters them each week. Green means the goal was met. Red means it was missed. The board shows 13 weeks, so a trend is plain to see.

An **Action** is small, has one owner, and is due in about a week. Actions are how decisions turn into work.

A **Blocker** is anything in the way: a problem, a risk, a question nobody has answered. Raising one is quick, so people do it as soon as they notice.

## The Weekly Meeting pulls it together

The Weekly Meeting has a fixed agenda. Each part reads from one of the pieces already described.

| Part of the meeting | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Check-in | A personal best and a business best from each person |
| Metrics Board Review | The team's metrics and their goals |
| Priority Review | The team's active Priorities for the current quarter |
| Updates | Good news and concerns, one line each |
| Action Review | Open Actions owned by people in the team |
| PSR | The team's open Blockers |
| Conclude | Ratings, Actions created in the meeting, and Key Messages |

The first five parts are quick reports. Anything that needs discussion waits for PSR, which gets most of the time. PSR stands for Problem, Solve, Resolve.

## How one thing becomes another

Several buttons turn one kind of record into the next, so nothing is retyped and nothing is lost.

| From | To | How |
|---|---|---|
| A missed number on a Metrics Board | A Blocker | Select the warning icon in the cell |
| An Update in a Weekly Meeting | A Blocker | Select the arrow icon next to the Update |
| An Action open for 14 days or more | A Blocker | Choose **Escalate to Blocker** in the Action's three-dot menu |
| A Blocker in PSR | An Action | Select the tick-box icon next to the Blocker |

Blockers and Priorities can be linked. Actions can be linked to a Priority, a Blocker, or both. The links show on each page, so you can trace an Action back to the result it serves.

## Why a rhythm

A fixed weekly agenda sounds rigid. In practice it removes decisions. Nobody plans the meeting. Nobody wonders where to raise a problem. The same parts come round each week, and each one shows the current state without anyone preparing slides.

The agenda and the sections of the Vision and Delivery Board are defaults, not rules. Each organisation can set its own.
